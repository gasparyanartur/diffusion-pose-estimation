# PoseFlow: ideas, setups and verdicts

Updated 17 November 2026. A reduced research review of the [complete historical audit](/home/gasparyanartur/dev/pose-graph-diff/docs/reports/all_run_results.md). Routine smoke tests, duplicate starts, implementation-only checks and repeated evaluation launches are omitted. Every substantive numbered family is retained: **V1, V3–V56 (55 families), plus unnumbered Codebook**. Meaningful controls and branches stay with their idea; V42 and V45 appear in more than one section because they contain distinct hypotheses.

**What the evidence says.** V5/V6 remain the strongest practical PoseFlow endpoints. DINO image reads have a promising endpoint; anatomical attention bias and repaired codebook conditioning show no gain. Residual person memory has a small matched benefit. Training on generated states sometimes helps, while extra inference calls have no consistent advantage. Most learned-latent and sequential-generation recipes remain weaker; the later V56 result is promising within its lineage but unfinished.

## Reading the results

- AP uses **0–100 points**. Results are **full official TEST, all 8,000 images**, expressed as **pose / bbox**. Box-only rows explicitly say `bbox`. No VAL or fixed32 number fills these detector results.
- Unless stated otherwise, resize is **256/427**, seed is 42, and K means denoiser calls. Sequential models use their native generation procedure. Epoch fractions describe **completed training / planned training**, not selected-checkpoint age. Important earlier selected ages are called out; exact checkpoint/update/selection rules are in each linked audit entry.
- Endpoints follow the audit’s frozen original best/final/latest rules. No retraining or new TEST checkpoint/sampler sweep was performed. Recent VAL remains a historical diagnostic under ED-Pose TRAIN+VAL pretraining overlap. Historical TEST monitoring/selection makes TEST a development benchmark.
- Comparisons across resolution, model size, training budget, selected age, confidence and postprocessing are qualified. Most experiments have one training seed. Compatible-source replays restore all trained weights but do not prove identity with unarchived original training code; full provenance remains in the audit.

Terms: **h** is carried person state; **DINO** supplies frozen pretrained image features; **OTA** allows multiple predictions to match a person during training; **DDIM** is the reverse-step sampler; **EMA** is an exponential moving average of target weights. Clean-code reconstruction receives geometry as input. Values round to two decimals; tiny positive AP is shown as `<0.01`.

## Conclusions that survive the reduction

1. **Strong earlier models:** V5 and V6 are the practical reference points at 256/427. V5 at paper resolution still trails the released native ED-Pose/DRP references.
2. **Specific controlled gains:** reduced-model OTA, V24 residual memory and V44 generated-state exposure have bounded positive results. Their interventions, budgets and scopes differ; none validates all later designs.
3. **Representation quality is not detector quality:** clean codec fidelity, four-image memorization and GT-source conditional recovery cannot replace Gaussian-start/image-only detection. Most tested learned-latent recipes remain weak despite useful diagnostics.
4. **More calls are not a demonstrated general advantage:** V40-EMA mitigates a multi-call loss, V42 JEPA worsens it, and V45-task JEPA partly recovers it while remaining below one-call AP.
5. **Sequential generation remains unresolved as a general idea:** several completed concrete recipes fail; proposal-grounded successors recover performance. V56 is still partial. Different capacity, pretrained transfer, objectives and checkpoint rules prevent reading this history as a controlled ranking of sequential mechanisms.
