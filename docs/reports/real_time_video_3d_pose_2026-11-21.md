# Real-time 3D human pose estimation from video

*Literature and implementation review through 21 November 2026. The main setting is one ordinary RGB camera. Values below come from the cited authors and their comparison tables. The review includes a static audit of released entry points.*

## Findings

- **One person, live camera-space joints or mesh.** InstantHMR-M has the clearest 30 FPS evidence in the literature reviewed. The authors sustained 30 FPS for 342 seconds on a Galaxy S23 with one dropped frame out of 10,409. The app tracks one person, redetects once a second and draws a 595-vertex mesh. On EMDB-1, the one-crop fit scored 45.2 mm PA-MPJPE and 70.9 mm MPJPE with benchmark boxes. The accuracy experiment and live run answer different questions. [InstantHMR v2, Tables 2, 16 and 17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09195v2)
- **Causal body-joint lifting from a stream of 2D detections.** PoseRWGCN reports 42.2 mm Human3.6M MPJPE and 3.0 ms median / 3.6 ms p90 per update on an RTX 4090, timed from 2D keypoints. MoViD reports a measured 66.2 ms RGB pose pipeline on a Jetson Orin NX, including 2D estimation and preprocessing. These are complementary results. [PoseRWGCN, Tables 1 and 5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-026-02239-x), [MoViD, Tables 4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03299)
- **Multiple people, persistent identities.** CoMotion, DETRAM and Multi-HMR 2 carry temporal information for association or recovery. Published rates are 5.68, 10.87 and up to 20 FPS respectively under their reported configurations. XNect demonstrated over 30 FPS earlier with a lighter body-capture pipeline and a different accuracy/output contract. The literature does not establish a shared 30 FPS accuracy frontier for detailed meshes and identity recovery in crowds. [CoMotion](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12186), [DETRAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09089), [Multi-HMR 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14841), [XNect](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00837)
- **World-space motion from a moving monocular camera.** OnlineHMR and Human3R are the main causal references found. OnlineHMR's comparison puts both well below 30 FPS, and the two trade off different trajectory metrics. With a calibrated camera rig, methods such as TEMPO and RapidPoseTriangulation already have near- or above-30 FPS evidence for 3D body joints. [OnlineHMR, Tables 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17355v1), [Human3R](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06219), [TEMPO](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Choudhury_TEMPO_Efficient_Multi-View_Pose_Estimation_Tracking_and_Forecasting_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf), [RapidPoseTriangulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21692)

## What “real-time” measures

A causal system can emit a pose for frame *t* from observations through *t*. A fixed-delay system can wait for a known number of future frames. For an input sampled at *f* Hz and *k* future frames, the wait alone is *k/f* seconds. At 30 Hz a centered 27-frame window adds 433 ms and a centered 243-frame window adds 4.03 s. Those figures assume consecutive frames and center output. A causal 243-frame receptive field imposes no future-frame wait, although its startup and recovery after a track reset deserve separate measurement. A batch that returns 243 poses quickly can report high throughput even when its first pose arrives seconds late. [VideoPose3D, Section 3 and Appendix A.1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Pavllo_3D_Human_Pose_Estimation_in_Video_With_Temporal_Convolutions_and_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf)

At 30 FPS a new frame arrives every 33.3 ms. A pipelined system can keep up even when any given frame spends longer in the system. A camera-to-pose claim should therefore give sustained output rate, dropped frames and latency percentiles from acquisition to the same frame's estimate. A per-crop runtime, a per-frame detector-to-pose runtime and the age of the rendered pose each measure a different interval.

The output also changes the task. **Root-relative** joints describe pose around the pelvis. **Camera-space** positions estimate where the person is relative to the camera. **World-space** trajectories also account for camera motion. Human3.6M Protocol 1 MPJPE is a root-relative joint error. PA-MPJPE uses a per-pose similarity alignment, so a low value provides limited evidence about metric position, scale or heading. PVE measures mesh vertices. For a moving camera, report root translation and world-aligned errors with the alignment specified. In monocular RGB, projecting a proportionally bigger body farther away can yield the same image, so learned scale and scene cues matter for metric localization. [VideoPose3D](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Pavllo_3D_Human_Pose_Estimation_in_Video_With_Temporal_Convolutions_and_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf), [OnlineHMR, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17355v1), [MeTRAbs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07227)

**The best same-study mobile comparison is InstantHMR v2, Tables 1–2.** With one crop and fit, InstantHMR-M gives 45.2 mm EMDB-1 PA-MPJPE at 6.40 ms on the S23 NPU. NLF-S gives 45.0 mm at 67.97 ms, and NLF-L gives 39.3 mm at 125 ms. The comparison uses a common EMDB-1 evaluation protocol except that NLF uses its own camera/crop handling as described in Section 4. On the RTX 4070 with TensorRT, NLF-L's corresponding per-crop time is 4.32 ms. The device and operators can change the practical ranking. NLF localizes requested points on a continuous body template, which also makes it a useful desktop accuracy reference. [InstantHMR v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09195v2), [NLF, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07532)

**The Android timing breakdown is especially informative.** In the five-minute replay, ordinary tracked frames took 12.73 ms median and 13.46 ms p95. The roughly 3% of frames with a detector call took 31.4 ms median and 33.8 ms p95. The capture experiment measured sustained live output on one subject doing squats, arm raises and turns. It used the smaller display mesh and a fixed detector cadence. The paper does not establish the same rate when reacquiring many people or processing dense meshes for every person. [InstantHMR v2, Appendix N](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09195v2)

**How far ahead is offline lifting?** SCR-Mamba-X (November 2026 preprint) reports 36.6 mm P1 with 243 detected Stacked Hourglass frames. The temporal computation uses neighboring future groups, so the score applies to a full clip. A comparison against PoseRWGCN's 42.2 mm illustrates the settings on offer but cannot assign the gap to lookahead because the architectures and training differ. The cleaner causal-versus-bidirectional comparisons are within VideoPose3D (49.0 versus 46.8 mm) and within PoseRWGCN (42.22 versus 41.91 mm). Glimpse reached 39.3 mm on a single Human3.6M frame by adding HRNet image features to CPN joints. It is an accuracy reference for keeping local image evidence, with no live pipeline result established here. [SCR-Mamba, Section 3.3 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.15526), [PoseRWGCN](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-026-02239-x), [VideoPose3D](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Pavllo_3D_Human_Pose_Estimation_in_Video_With_Temporal_Convolutions_and_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf), [Glimpse, ICML 2026](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tang26p.html)

## Multi-person tracking and world-space recovery

**CoMotion** updates persistent people directly from shared frame features, with separate queries for people entering the scene. **DETRAM** unifies detection, track updates and mesh recovery with a temporal memory bank and optional user prompts. In DETRAM's PoseTrack21 table under the same ignore-region fix, automatic DETRAM scores 73.6 MOTA and 80.6 IDF1 at 10.87 FPS, versus CoMotion at 71.4 and 79.5 at 5.68 FPS. These are identity/detection scores. They do not establish an error in millimetres during occlusion. The higher prompted results use ground-truth boxes to simulate missed-person prompts. CoMotion's “strict” row raises the detection confidence threshold and has a separate recall/precision trade-off. CoMotion also reports 60.0 mm root-relative MPJPE on full 3DPW frames in a separate single-image evaluation. In its initialized-track ablation, removing the learned state increases ID switches from 491 to 559 with little change on 3DPW, suggesting that identity and pose error can respond differently to temporal memory. [DETRAM, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09089), [CoMotion, Tables 1–4 and Appendix A.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12186)

**Multi-HMR 2, also called MuCHeR**, predicts several people and their camera-space meshes in one frame, then associates detections against recent memory with distilled SAM 2 features. Its paper reports 4–5 FPS unoptimized and 20 FPS after PyTorch compilation on V100. **RTMW3D** is a framewise reference when the output can be 133 whole-body landmarks and identity is handled separately. **MeTRAbs** estimates metric-scale skeletons and camera-frame placement from crops and intrinsics, with a speed/accuracy knob in test-time augmentation. The authors' original MeTRAbs timing excludes person detection. Neither per-frame body localization nor persistent camera-relative IDs supplies a shared moving-camera world trajectory. [Multi-HMR 2, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14841), [RTMW3D](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.08634), [MeTRAbs paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07227), [MeTRAbs timing slides](https://istvansarandi.com/media/sarandi_metrabs_slides.pdf)

**OnlineHMR** keeps a history-only key/value cache for local mesh inference and uses human-masked incremental SLAM for camera motion. **Human3R** extends a recurrent scene reconstruction model with multi-person body queries so that scene, camera and people use shared history. Its own paper reports 15 FPS for ViT-S/672 on a 4090 and 5 FPS for ViT-L/896. OnlineHMR's separate same-video comparison gives Human3R 4.8 FPS and OnlineHMR 3.3 FPS on an RTX 6000 Ada. These are different published setups. The available measurements do not isolate why the Human3R rates differ. [Human3R, Appendix A.4–A.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06219), [OnlineHMR, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17355v1)

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TRACE | 529.0 | 1,702.3 | 17.7 | 24.6 |

WA aligns the whole 100-frame segment. W estimates the alignment from the first two frames, then scores the segment. OnlineHMR has the lower segment-aligned joint error and Human3R the lower first-two-frame-aligned error. WHAM is an important example of a causal body estimator whose evaluated world-space setup with DPVO can revise past camera poses using later frames. GVHMR's network speed also excludes upstream tracking, feature extraction and camera motion preprocessing in its project timing. [OnlineHMR, Tables 2–3 and §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17355v1), [GVHMR project](https://zju3dv.github.io/gvhmr/)

## Calibrated multiview

**TEMPO** builds a time-aware voxel representation around tracked people. In its CMU Panoptic comparison the ResNet-50 configuration gives **14.68 mm MPJPE at 29.3 FPS** on an A100. The HRNet configuration scores 15.99 mm at 20.3 FPS. Its transfer table also gives 62.96 mm on Human3.6M when trained on Panoptic alone. That difference shows why the in-domain number is a poor standalone forecast for a different rig. [TEMPO, Tables 1 and 3](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Choudhury_TEMPO_Efficient_Multi-View_Pose_Estimation_Tracking_and_Forecasting_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)

**RapidPoseTriangulation** associates 2D detections across views and triangulates after pruning inconsistent candidates. In the 5-camera Panoptic setup it reports **30.6 mm at 57.5 FPS** on RTX 3090, and **23.9 mm at 29.6 FPS** with 10 cameras. Its roughly 0.1 ms Shelf timing measures the CPU 2D-to-3D stage alone. **TwinPose** (SIGGRAPH 2026) uses separate reconstruction subspaces per person and supports multiple 2D detectors. Its 0.92 ms Shelf number likewise belongs to an association/reconstruction comparison. Neither isolated geometry timing describes sensing, synchronization and detector latency. **CoDePose** reports 12.1 mm on Human3.6M with four views, 27 frames and ViTPose input. That is an accuracy reference under a different camera and temporal protocol. [RapidPoseTriangulation, Tables 2, 7 and 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21692), [TwinPose paper and project](https://zgspose.github.io/TwinPose/), [CoDePose, ECCV 2026](https://media.eventhosts.cc/Conferences/ECCV2026/pdfs/11345.pdf)

## Released inference entry points

- **InstantHMR Python.** The pinned desktop demo has a camera path, calls the pose pipeline per frame and exposes detector stride and optional temporal filters. This is a different executable from the Android benchmark. For NLF, the published video pipeline runs segmentation and tracking before pose/shape recovery, so the fast NLF-S/L crop graph has a different timing boundary. [InstantHMR demo](https://github.com/Naturalpad-studio/InstantHMR/blob/6b187eada5cf1deac6a2eaa1a85edcad7bfba660/demo.py#L60), [NLF video pipeline](https://github.com/isarandi/nlf-pipeline)
- **MoViD.** The release has a separate stream entry point that opens a camera, calls the detector inside the frame loop and invokes its stateful inference method. Its similarly named offline demo precomputes/caches 2D tracks before reconstruction. The edge demo that matches the streaming claim is `MoViD_edge/real_time.py`. [Camera-ready instructions](https://github.com/HKUST-MINSys-Lab/MoViD/blob/6f0030f86afa7699ce5a76a8822e8ff2adaff1a8/README.md#L113), [live frame loop](https://github.com/HKUST-MINSys-Lab/MoViD/blob/6f0030f86afa7699ce5a76a8822e8ff2adaff1a8/MoViD_edge/real_time.py#L674)
- **OnlineHMR.** The documented public script accepts a file and extracts frames before the reconstruction loop. The causal architecture makes an incremental runtime possible. The released demo entry point is evidence of a video-file workflow. [Release instructions](https://github.com/Tsukasane/Video-OnlineHMR/blob/5236d2206068027e1b73480e34903092728f84f5/README.md#L109), [frame extraction](https://github.com/Tsukasane/Video-OnlineHMR/blob/5236d2206068027e1b73480e34903092728f84f5/scripts/run_custom.py#L465)

## What a matched comparison would resolve

The most informative comparison for monocular RGB would pair **InstantHMR-M with one-crop fit**, **NLF-S or NLF-L** and a **causal 2D-to-3D pipeline**. Evaluate the lifter on the same 2D detections when measuring lifting, then time each complete pipeline from the same paced input. EMDB-1 offers mesh/joint accuracy, EMDB-2 tests moving-camera trajectories, and a crowd sequence is needed to measure missed poses and ID switches. Include one-person and crowded settings, fast turns, limb occlusion and re-entry, with time-to-reacquisition alongside accuracy. Per-person MPJPE should be accompanied by detection coverage so that dropping difficult people does not improve the reported result silently. Log input FPS and resolution, person count, crop source, detector cadence, temporal lookahead, precision, checkpoint and runtime, plus sustained throughput and p50/p95 capture-to-pose age. Retain each model's output-frame timestamp. [EMDB protocol in OnlineHMR](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17355v1), [InstantHMR benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09195v2), [PoseTrack21 tracking in DETRAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09089)

For the existing [pose research](3d_human_pose_literature_audit_2026-11-05.md), the closest precedent for carrying proposals between frames is CoMotion's distinction between updating existing people from current pixels and detecting new people. PoseRWGCN provides a narrower history-only geometry baseline. For a study of temporal memory, the evidence would come from carry-versus-reset with identical image features, detections and latency budgets, scored on occlusion, re-entry and identity switches as well as joint error. This review used primary papers, supplements, author technical reports and selected code. It is a targeted scan with published measurements and static inspection. No local runtime or accuracy benchmark was executed.
